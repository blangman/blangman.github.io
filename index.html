About

I recently graduated from Harvard College with a double major in Mathematics and Government, bouncing between courses like Real Analysis and AI Policy. Extracurricularly, I was the President of Harvard Tech for Social Good, where I oversaw the end-to-end creation of technical projects for high-impact nonprofits, and a research assistant on Kosuke Imai's ALARM project, which focused on quantitative gerrymandering research. Professionally, I worked as a full-stack developer intern at IBM over my sophomore and junior summers.

I am also a huge fan of comedy — specifically comedy writing. In my time at school, I was a staff writer at The Harvard Lampoon, the oldest continuously published humor magazine in the country. My senior year, an original musical I cowrote was chosen by the Hasty Pudding Theatricals, the oldest theatrical organization in the country, to be their 177th annual show, have a five-week residency in Cambridge, and tour New York City and Bermuda. Currently, I'm developing a parody novel to be published by Penguin Random House, and my work was just featured in the September issue of The Hollywood Reporter (see the pieces).

Overall, I'm a generalist with a multidisciplinary background and multidisciplinary interests. I excel at working with people, and I have a ton of experience collaborating on projects of varying complexity, often on a deadline. I credit this most to the fact that I am a deeply passionate person and a fast learner — if I care about something, I go all in every time.
